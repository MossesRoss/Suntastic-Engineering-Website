How this is calculated
  • Tariff: Tamil Nadu domestic slabs on a 2-month basis. The first 200 units are free if you use 500 or fewer units, otherwise the first 100 are free. Then ₹4.70, ₹6.30, ₹8.40, ₹9.45, ₹10.50 and ₹11.55 per unit in rising slabs. Kerala uses the KSERC December 2024 slabs, with flat (non-telescopic) billing above 250 units a month. Your bill amount is converted to units using these slabs. Energy charges only; fixed charges, electricity duty and fuel surcharge stay on your bill after solar.
  • Net metering: you pay for imported minus exported units. Surplus units are given no value, so oversizing adds cost without adding savings. A network charge on Tamil Nadu generation (about ₹0.32 per unit generated) is deducted.
  • Solar yield: 1,350 to 1,450 units per kW per year depending on region, after soiling, temperature and inverter losses, with 0.5% yearly degradation.
  • Subsidy: PM Surya Ghar pays ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the 3rd kW, capped at ₹78,000. The Tamil Nadu top-up is ₹5,000, ₹10,000 or ₹22,000 for systems up to 1 kW, up to 2 kW, and 3 kW and above, for residential portal registrations on or after 5 Aug 2026. Kerala state subsidy is not included.
  • Cost and returns: indicative turnkey price before site survey. Tariffs are held flat, running costs are ₹600 per kW per year, and the inverter is replaced in year 12. Your formal quote after a site survey will replace these estimates.
  • Roof area: 80 to 100 sq.ft per kW including row spacing and walkways.
